CivicPriorities is feasible only if treated like an air-traffic control prototype, not a miniature government. The missing question is not “can AI classify requests?” but “what prevents a persuasive ranking from becoming fake evidence?”

Score it against the rubric with hard gates:

- AI execution, 25: 20 if Gemini extracts bounded fields with source spans, abstains, and survives provider failure; 5 only for STT/translation. No credit for generic chat.
- Deployability, 20: 10 for one container and isolated sessions; 5 for versioned country adapters; 5 for reproducible fixtures. Brazil proves contract portability, not BRICS deployment.
- India depth, 20: full credit requires two Indian configurations, population denominators, infrastructure gaps, investment plans, language provenance, and boundary/time validation. Synthetic joins must be visibly synthetic.
- Problem fit, 20: full credit only when project recommendations, budget/overlap constraints, sensitivity, and review are demonstrated. Ticket routing scores near zero.
- Impact, 15: award mostly for accountable decision support, not claimed outcomes.

Required contracts: every request, region, metric, investment, and project must carry country, code system, unit, time, version, source mode, source ID, and missingness. Ranking must abstain—not impute—when joins, denominators, boundary versions, or time bases are incompatible. Duplicate submissions cannot equal votes. AI may explain validated calculations, never perform them.

Retain voice/text, editable transcripts, messaging import, demographic/infrastructure/investment joins, hotspots, policy recommendations, DPG-style open contracts, and national-scale architecture. Cut decorative 3D, broad localization, live WhatsApp, and unverified deployment language.
